Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain and apply the core ideas behind Evolution, reversibility, consequences in both exam questions and real architecture discussions.

Official alignmentLG 01-03 — Long-term Impact of Architecture Decisions.

Architecture decisions create future constraints

Important architecture decisions shape what becomes easy, difficult, cheap, or expensive later. A database strategy, deployment model, or service boundary can become a long-lived constraint.

Decision today → Dependencies → Future change cost → Long-term consequences

Requirements and environments change

Architecture is affected by changes in requirements, technology, regulation, teams, traffic, and operating environments. A good architecture is not one that never changes; it is one that can evolve without uncontrolled cost.

Reversibility as a decision heuristic

Useful Staff heuristicThe more expensive a decision is to reverse, the more evidence and explicit reasoning it deserves.
  • Choosing a local helper library: usually easy to reverse.
  • Splitting a monolith into 40 services: expensive to reverse.
  • Choosing a company-wide identity model: extremely consequential.

Architecture and business influence each other

Software architecture supports business processes, but software capabilities can also reshape business processes. Architects should therefore understand the business context, not only technical structure.

Common CPSA-F exam traps

  1. Architectural decisions never need revisiting. — Treat this as suspicious or false unless the context explicitly supports it.
  2. The future can be predicted accurately enough to design for every possibility. — Treat this as suspicious or false unless the context explicitly supports it.
  3. A technically good decision has no business consequences. — Treat this as suspicious or false unless the context explicitly supports it.

Key terms to remember

ReversibilityHow easy or expensive a decision is to undo.
Architectural consequenceA positive or negative effect caused by an architecture decision.
EvolutionControlled change of a software system over time.

Practical exercise

A SaaS invoicing platform has 5,000 customers in Germany, runs in AWS eu-central-1, uses a modular monolith with PostgreSQL, and has 6 developers. The company may grow to 500,000 EU customers in three years. The CTO wants 30 microservices immediately.

Identify:

  • 3 architecture decisions that may become problematic later,
  • 3 decisions you would avoid making now,
  • 1 decision that is highly reversible.

Review questions

Why do architecture decisions have long-term impact?

They shape dependencies, quality attributes, and the cost of future change.

Should architecture optimize for every possible future?

No. Overdesign adds cost and complexity. Design for known drivers and credible risks.

What is a useful heuristic for important decisions?

Consider reversibility: expensive-to-reverse choices deserve stronger evidence.
